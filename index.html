A machine that builds itself

This is the bacterial flagellar motor: a real rotary engine about 45 nanometres across. A thousand of them would fit across the width of a human hair.

Nobody builds it. Around 25 kinds of protein drift and jiggle through the cell until they click into place, in exactly the right order. The core motor is finished in under half an hour.

Then the tail grows from its tip: up to 20,000 flagellin proteins are fed up a hollow core just 2 nm wide and snap on, one by one, at the far end.

Powered by protons flowing into the cell, it spins up to 300 times a second and can slam into reverse in milliseconds. A single E. coli runs several of them at once.
